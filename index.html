Ongoing Final Year Project

Fabrication and Mechanical Characterization of Hybrid Natural Fibre Composite Reinforced with Natural Fillers

Fabrication and mechanical characterization of hybrid natural fibre composite materials reinforced with natural fillers, aimed at developing eco-friendly composites with improved mechanical properties and reduced environmental impact.

Overview

This final-year major project investigates hybrid natural fibre composites as a sustainable alternative to synthetic-fibre reinforced materials. The composite is fabricated by reinforcing a biodegradable polymer matrix with Jute and Hemp fibre, while incorporating Cashew Friction Dust, Egg Shell Powder and Rice Husk Powder as natural fillers — converting low-value agricultural and industrial by-products into a value-added engineering material with comparable mechanical performance and a lower environmental footprint than conventional composites.

Materials Used

Jute Fibre Hemp Fibre Cashew Friction Dust Egg Shell Powder Rice Husk Powder PLA Resin (bio-polymer matrix)

Objectives

  • Develop a sustainable hybrid natural fibre composite using renewable fibres and natural fillers
  • Improve mechanical properties through optimized material composition
  • Utilize agricultural and industrial waste as value-added reinforcing materials
  • Reduce dependence on synthetic fibre composites and support green manufacturing
  • Assess the composite for applications in automotive, construction, furniture, packaging and other lightweight engineering products
  • Promote environmentally responsible material development with potential for industrial commercialization

Newness / Uniqueness

Unlike conventional composites that primarily use synthetic reinforcements such as glass or carbon fibres, this project combines Jute and Hemp fibre with three natural waste-derived fillers in a biodegradable polymer matrix. The optimized fibre-filler composition is designed to enhance interfacial bonding, reduce material weight and improve mechanical performance compared to conventional natural fibre composites, while effectively converting agricultural and industrial by-products into engineering materials.

Process Flow

  1. Selection of natural fibres — Hemp + Jute
  2. Selection of natural fillers — Eggshell Powder, Rice Husk, Cashew Friction Dust
  3. Fibre preparation — cleaning, drying & alkali treatment
  4. Preparation of matrix — resin + hardener
  5. Mixing of natural fillers with the matrix
  6. Fabrication of composite — hand lay-up / compression moulding
  7. Curing process — room temperature / hot press
  8. Specimen preparation — as per ASTM standards
  9. Mechanical testing — tensile, flexural, impact, hardness
  10. Result analysis & performance comparison
  11. Conclusion & industrial applications — automotive, construction, furniture, packaging, etc.

Testing Methodology

  • Tensile testing — UTM, per ASTM standards
  • Flexural testing — three-point bend test
  • Impact testing — Izod / Charpy
  • Hardness testing — Shore / Rockwell as applicable

Potential Applications

  • Automotive — interior panels, door trims, dashboards, luggage compartments, non-structural components
  • Construction — partition panels, ceiling boards, wall claddings, insulation materials, decorative components
  • Furniture — tabletops, cabinets, chairs, shelves, modular furniture
  • Packaging — biodegradable protective packaging, pallets, reusable containers
  • Consumer goods — casings, storage boxes, household products, lifestyle accessories
  • Agriculture — equipment covers, seedling trays, storage bins, utility products

Market Potential

Growing global demand for sustainable, lightweight and biodegradable engineering materials is driving industries to replace conventional glass-fibre and plastic-based composites. The use of locally available natural fibres and waste materials creates opportunities for MSMEs, rural enterprises and agro-based industries, promoting value addition to agricultural by-products and supporting local employment. The technology can be scaled from laboratory-level production to industrial manufacturing using conventional composite fabrication techniques, giving it strong commercial viability and export potential.
